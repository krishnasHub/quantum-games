HOW IT WORKS

You are a quantum particle. Until something finds you, you are not in one place: you are a wave, spread over everywhere you might be. The glow on screen is that wave (strictly, |ψ|², the chance of finding you at each point).

Each level is a puzzle. Place parts (mirrors, walls, watchers), choose how you launch, and press Play. The wave flows out of the source and spreads through the level. The exit is a detector: any of the wave that flows into it is caught. Your score is the share caught at the exit. That is exactly the chance that, when the particle is finally found, it is found at the exit: in 70 out of 100 universes, you made it. No dice are rolled; the same setup always scores the same.

Everything comes from a real simulation of the Schrödinger equation. Nothing is scripted, so the physics below is the puzzle.

Waves go round things

A wave bends around anything smaller than itself. That is why mirrors and walls are three tiles long: a one-tile mirror would let most of you slip past.

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

A wave cannot be both narrow and have a single wavelength. So the more sharply your position is defined (Δx), the more spread out your momentum is (Δp), and Δx·Δp never drops below ħ/2. A narrow packet fans out quickly. A wide one holds together.

Tunnelling

A barrier taller than your energy would stop a ball every time. A wave decays inside the barrier instead of stopping dead, and if the barrier is thin, a little of it comes out the other side.

Interference

Where the wave meets itself, after bouncing off walls or splitting around an obstacle, it adds up in some places and cancels in others. The ripples and stripes you see are real.

The quantum Zeno effect

A trap is a detector that catches you. A watcher who never stops looking is different: each instant it checks "is the particle here?", almost nothing has leaked in since the last check, so it almost never finds you, and the wave is pushed back out. In the limit of constant watching (Zeno dynamics), the watcher's line of sight acts exactly like a wall. A watched pot never boils.

What is real and what is tuned

  • Real: the Schrödinger evolution (split-step Fourier method, ħ = m = 1, lengths in grid cells), tunnelling, interference, the uncertainty bound, and the probabilities that make up your score.
  • Tuned: the world is 2D and 256 × 128 cells; detectors absorb with soft edges; a watcher's gaze uses the continuous-watching limit.
  • Not possible in reality: seeing the wave. A real experimenter only ever sees where the particle was found. The glow is the game letting you peek at the math.

CONTROLS

Pick a part
Click it in the Parts panel, or press 1–4
Place
Click the field. A green ghost means it fits; red means it doesn't
Turn
Click a placed part, or press Q / E (or use the wheel) before placing
Remove
Right-click a placed part, or use the Remove tool
Space
Play, or stop and go back to building
F
Run three times as fast
P
Phase view: colour shows the wave's phase
H
Show the hint
Esc
Back to the title screen

One star clears a level. Your parts stay where you left them, so you can tweak and run again as often as you like.
